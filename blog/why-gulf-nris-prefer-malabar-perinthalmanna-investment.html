Why Gulf NRIs Prefer Malabar & Perinthalmanna for Real Estate Investment

September 21, 2026 · 8 min read · By Nalakath Holdings

Most Kerala NRI investment guides talk about Kochi, Thrissur, or Trivandrum. Almost none talk about Malabar — Malappuram, Kozhikode, Kannur, Kasaragod and Wayanad — despite it having one of the highest concentrations of Gulf-remittance households anywhere in the state. Here's why that's a gap, and why Gulf NRIs with roots in this region are increasingly looking past land and flats toward income-generating investment closer to home.

1. Malabar's Gulf connection runs deeper than the rest of Kerala

Malappuram district in particular has one of Kerala's highest per-capita NRI populations, with multi-generational ties to UAE, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Oman and Bahrain. Entire towns in this corridor are economically shaped by Gulf remittances — it's not a market that needs to be introduced to NRI investment, it's one that's been quietly funding it for decades, mostly informally, through family land purchases and home construction.

2. Calicut International Airport changes the calculation

Perinthalmanna sits roughly 25km from Calicut International Airport — meaning a Gulf-based investor can fly in, inspect a property, and fly back within a short trip, something that matters far more than it sounds once you're actually planning a visit. Compare this to the multi-hour drive from Kochi or Trivandrum airports to more central Kerala real estate markets.

3. The gap: capital has gone into land, not income

Here's the part most Kerala investment guides skip. Gulf remittance capital into Malabar has overwhelmingly gone into two places: land banking (buy and hold, hope for appreciation) and home construction (personal use, not investment). What's been largely absent is organized, income-generating investment — the kind that pays you back while you hold it, not just when you sell.

This is precisely the gap Oval Palace Resort is built to fill — a structured, LLP-based hospitality investment in Perinthalmanna, targeting 12% annual returns through resort operations (rooms, F&B, events), not land appreciation or personal-use construction.

5. What this means if you have roots in Malabar

If your family is from Malappuram, Kozhikode, Kannur, Kasaragod or Wayanad and you've been sending money home for land or construction, it's worth asking a different question: is there a way for that capital to also generate income while you hold it, rather than sitting as an appreciating but idle asset? For Gulf NRIs with Malabar roots specifically, a local hospitality investment does something a Kochi flat or an out-of-state mutual fund can't — it puts capital to work in the region your family is actually from, run by people who understand it.

Kerala's NRI investment conversation has centred on Kochi and Trivandrum for years. Malabar's Gulf-remittance economy deserves the same organized investment options — it's just been waiting for someone to build them.
