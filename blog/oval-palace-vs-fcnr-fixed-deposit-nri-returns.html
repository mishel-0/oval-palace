Oval Palace Resort vs FCNR Fixed Deposit: NRI Returns Compared

September 21, 2026 · 7 min read · By Nalakath Holdings

If you're weighing an NRI investment fcnr returns comparison against a hospitality or resort investment, you're really asking one question: how much of my NRI portfolio should sit in guaranteed bank returns versus growth assets? Here's a plain-numbers look at both, so you can decide where Oval Palace Resort fits in your own NRI portfolio investment scheme — not instead of an FCNR deposit, but alongside one.

1. What an FCNR deposit actually gives you

An FCNR (B) — Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) — deposit lets you hold a fixed deposit in India in a foreign currency (USD, GBP, EUR, AED is not directly supported but AED-earners commonly convert to USD/EUR), avoiding rupee exchange-rate risk on the principal. As of 2026, after the RBI lifted its rate cap, published FCNR rates across major Indian banks range roughly from the mid-4% to just over 7% per annum, depending on the bank, currency, and tenure — and interest earned is tax-free in India.

The case for FCNR: capital is effectively guaranteed by the bank, the return is fixed and known in advance, there's no market or business risk, and it's fully and freely repatriable. It's the closest thing to a risk-free NRI investment.

2. What a hospitality investment like Oval Palace Resort targets instead

A resort unit investment is structurally different — you're not lending money to a bank, you're holding a partnership stake in an operating hospitality business (rooms, F&B, events) via a registered LLP. Oval Palace Resort units are structured to target 12% annual returns, well above FCNR rates, because the return is tied to occupancy and hospitality revenue rather than a bank's fixed promise.

The trade-off: this is not a guaranteed, government-insured product. Returns depend on the resort operating successfully — occupancy, F&B performance, and overall demand. It carries genuine business risk that an FCNR deposit does not.

4. So which should you choose?

Neither, exclusively — that's the honest answer. Most NRI investors we speak with don't treat this as an either/or decision. FCNR deposits protect capital and provide predictable liquidity; a hospitality investment like Oval Palace Resort is the growth allocation sitting alongside it. Where the right split falls depends on your own risk appetite, time horizon, and how much of your portfolio is already sitting in fixed-income instruments.

An FCNR deposit answers "how do I protect capital?" A resort investment answers "how do I grow it?" Most well-built NRI portfolios need both.
