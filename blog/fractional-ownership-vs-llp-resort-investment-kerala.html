Fractional Ownership vs LLP Investment: Key Differences

August 25, 2026 · 7 min read · By Nalakath Holdings

"Fractional ownership" has become a popular term in Indian real estate over the last few years — and NRI investors researching resort or hospitality investment in Kerala will run into it, alongside newer terms like SM REITs and older, more familiar structures like LLP partnerships. They sound similar but work quite differently. Here's a clear breakdown of each.

1. What is fractional ownership, exactly?

Fractional ownership means multiple investors each own a "fraction" of a single high-value asset — commonly a commercial property, warehouse, or luxury real estate — and share in its rental income and appreciation proportionally to their stake. The appeal is simple: it lets investors access assets that would otherwise require far more capital than any one person wants to commit alone.

Until recently, most fractional ownership in India happened through Fractional Ownership Platforms (FOPs) — private companies that pooled investor money into a Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) that held the underlying property. These platforms operated largely outside any dedicated regulatory framework.

2. Enter SM REITs — the regulated version

In 2024, the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) introduced Small and Medium REITs (SM REITs) specifically to bring fractional ownership under formal regulatory oversight. Key features of the SM REIT framework:

  • SEBI-regulated — governed under updated REIT Regulations, not left to individual platform terms
  • Minimum investment of around ₹10 lakh per scheme
  • Minimum 200 investors required per scheme
  • Assets typically valued ₹50-500 crore — usually income-generating commercial property (offices, retail, warehouses, data centers)
  • At least 95% of the portfolio must be in revenue-generating, completed properties (not under-construction assets)
  • 100% of net distributable cash flow must be paid out to investors every quarter
  • Borrowing capped at 49% of asset value

SM REITs are a genuinely useful, regulator-backed option for investors who specifically want exposure to large commercial real estate through a formal, SEBI-supervised vehicle.

3. Where LLP-based resort investment fits

A unit-based partnership investment in a registered LLP — the structure behind Oval Palace Resort LLP — is a different, older, and equally well-established category: it is not an SM REIT, and doesn't claim to be. It's governed by India's Limited Liability Partnership Act and the specific partnership agreement each investor signs, rather than SEBI's REIT regulations.

In practice, it works in a similar spirit to fractional ownership — multiple partners each hold units in one asset and share its returns — while being a fundamentally different legal structure. Here's how the two compare directly:

RegulatorSEBI-regulatedGoverned by LLP Act & partnership agreement
Typical assetCompleted commercial propertyHospitality / resort development, often pre-completion

4. Which one is right for you?

SM REITs suit investors who want: exposure to large, already-completed commercial real estate, a formally SEBI-regulated structure, and quarterly cash distributions from day one.

LLP resort investment suits investors who want: a direct stake in a hospitality development from an earlier stage, structured pre-operational yields while the project completes, and post-operational revenue sharing once it opens — the model most NRI investors in Kerala hospitality assets are already familiar with.

Neither is inherently "better" — they're different tools for different goals. What matters is understanding which structure you're actually investing in, what regulatory framework (if any) governs it, and how your returns are generated.

Ask any developer offering a "fractional" or "unit-based" investment one direct question: is this an SEBI-regulated SM REIT, or a partnership structure governed by a private agreement? Both can be legitimate — but you should always know which one you're signing up for.
